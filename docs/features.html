Features

Six halls, one well

Mimir is organised as six views. You will spend most of your time in the Oracle, but the rest exist because studying is not only asking questions — it is being tested, being scored, and being told what you are avoiding.

ᛞ The Oracle

Streaming chat with your local model. Tokens arrive as they are generated, quizzes and flashcards render inline, and you can copy, edit, or regenerate any message.

ᛏ Trials

Four testing formats — multiple choice, written answer, flashcards, and a timed mock exam under real conditions.

ᚱ The Reckoning

Confidence per topic, all-time accuracy, current streak, learning velocity sparklines, a study heatmap, and a predicted grade.

ᚦ The Fates

Your revision schedule. Import a syllabus, set the exam date, and the plan is built backwards from the deadline.

ᛊ Chronicle

Every past session, paginated and fully searchable. Reload any conversation back into the Oracle to carry on.

ᚠ Scrolls

Your uploaded library. PDFs and images parsed, chunked, embedded, and searchable — with past papers detected automatically.

Conversation

Seven ways to be taught

Switch mode mid-conversation from the input bar. The same question answered in DEEP and in SWIFT are genuinely different answers, not the same text at two lengths.

ModeRuneWhat changes
DEEPᛞFull professor treatment — intuition, mechanics, worked example, checkpoint question
SWIFTᛊTwo to four sentences. No preamble, no follow-up menu
BASICᚱAssumes zero prior knowledge; analogy before any technical term
EXAMᛏHigh-yield only, plus the mistake students actually lose marks on
CODEᚲImplementation-level — data types, control flow, edge cases
MATHᛜDerivation first, from first principles, rendered in LaTeX
ODINᚨSocratic. It refuses to answer and asks until you get there

Quick actions

  • SCROLL — summarise a document into study notes
  • LESSON — a five-stage structured tutor session
  • TRIAL — generate a quiz on the spot
  • RUNES — build a flashcard deck
  • FATES — draft a revision schedule
  • MAP — render a mind map of a topic
  • WEB — allow a live search for this answer

In the chat itself

  • KaTeX maths — real rendered notation, not ASCII
  • Diagram reading — paste an image and it extracts every label and relationship
  • Stop mid-stream — cancel a generation you no longer want
  • Hover actions — copy, edit, or regenerate any message
  • Command palette — Ctrl+K for navigation and shortcuts
  • Honest errors — a fix command, not a stack trace
Memory

It does not forget what you fed it

Hybrid retrieval

Three ranking signals, fused. Vector similarity finds meaning, BM25 finds exact terms, Reciprocal Rank Fusion merges them, and a cross-encoder reranks the survivors.

  • Always-on user memory — document summaries and known struggles injected every turn, never gated by a similarity score
  • Nightly summariser — old sessions compressed into durable memory instead of discarded
  • Misconception tracking — topics you repeatedly fail are surfaced to the model deliberately

Scrolls — your library

  • PDF extraction — heading-aware structure via PyMuPDF
  • Image understanding — a vision model describes it, OCR as fallback
  • Semantic chunking — paragraph-aware, with sentence-boundary overlap so retrieval never cuts a key sentence in half
  • Past-paper detection — upload an exam paper and mark allocations are parsed automatically; a 2-mark question gets a short answer, an 8-mark one gets a full structure
  • Search and reassign — move a file to another subject without re-uploading
Testing & progress

Scored, not guessed

Four ways to be tested

  • Multiple choice — interactive cards, immediate right/wrong, full score card
  • Written answer — type freely; graded against mark points
  • Flashcards — Anki-style flip deck
  • Timed mock exam — countdown, then a per-question breakdown by marks
  • AI Examiner — supply a question and mark scheme, get examiner feedback line by line on what you earned and what you missed

What it tracks

  • SM-2 spaced repetition — ease factors and intervals updated after every single answer
  • Due-today queue — overdue topics one click away
  • Learning velocity — per-topic improvement slope, so a topic in free-fall is visible before the exam
  • Predicted grade — forgetting-curve decay plus score trajectory, with a confidence level and trend
  • Study heatmap — daily intensity across every subject
  • PDF export — take the whole report with you
Understanding structure

Why you are stuck, not just that you are

ᚷ Knowledge graph

A prerequisite map per subject. Click a topic to see what it rests on and what rests on it — usually the real blocker is two nodes upstream of the thing you keep failing.

ᚹ Mind maps

Generate a concept map for any topic or uploaded document, with node sizing driven by prominence in the source.

ᚠ Formula sheets

Formulas and definitions pulled out of your own notes, per subject, always one click away in the sidebar.

ᚺ Pomodoro

A floating timer with configurable work and break intervals that minimises out of the way instead of nagging.

ᛒ Exam countdown

Set the date and the Ragnarök counter in the right panel is real — and it drives how the revision plan is built.

ᛜ System tray

Minimises to tray. Ctrl+Shift+M summons it from anywhere on the desktop.
